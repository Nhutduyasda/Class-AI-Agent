# Sound design & music mixing
1. Select a restrained, upbeat or curious instrumental bed matching the narrative arc; avoid vocals and intense bass. Silence is better than inappropriate music.
2. Commercial safety: source track from an officially licensed provider/platform and keep original track filename, exact title/creator, source URL, downloaded license/receipt, applicable use, attribution terms and timestamp in music-license-manifest.json. Do not assume 'royalty free' means usable in any context. If no usable license, do not include a third-party song.
3. For a fully offline/no-cost technical demo, compose a subtle original instrumental bed with synthesis and log that it was algorithmically generated by the project; no borrowed melody or samples. Optional tiny soft pop/whoosh SFX synthesized from sine/noise for wrong→correct reveal. Do not use fake copyrighted sounds.
4. Voice first: loudness-normalize narration to approximately -16 LUFS with conservative true peak (<= -1.5 dBTP); mix the bed initially about 18–25 dB quieter than narration and reduce during speech. Tune by listening; numeric targets are starting points, not proof of intelligibility.
5. Add 150–400 ms fades at boundaries, no clicks, no abrupt stop, background continues over transitions and resolves at ending.
6. Report integrated loudness, true peak, measured speech-vs-music levels, audio codecs. Save separate voice.wav, music.wav, mixed.wav and final.mp4 for reproducibility.
7. Check mono smartphone listen, headphones and muted captions viewing. Stop as NOT_APPROVED if music masks words, feels generic/cheap, or does not fit.
